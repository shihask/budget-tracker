# Era file — Uruk, southern Mesopotamia, c. 3300–3100 BCE (Late Uruk IV → Uruk III / Jemdet Nasr)

Every prompt and every review is checked against this file. Each item is tagged:
**[D]** documented (source in `../references.md`) · **[I]** scholarly interpretation, debated ·
**[P]** plausible reconstruction, our choice · **[X]** banned (anachronism or unsupported).

## Writing and accounting — the core of EP01
- [D] Proto-cuneiform tablets from Uruk's Eanna precinct, the large majority administrative accounts
  (grain, rations, animals, labour, textiles).
- [D] **Numbers are impressed, not written.** The blunt round end of a reed stylus, pushed in at a
  slant, leaves a D-shaped / fingernail notch (the unit); pushed straight down it leaves a round
  dent (the ten). Larger values use a thicker stylus: big notches, big circles, a circle inside a
  notch. Two stylus thicknesses.
- [D] Non-numerical signs are **incised pictographs with curved lines** (e.g. an ear of barley),
  drawn with the pointed end. Not wedges.
- [D] Tablets: small (palm-sized, roughly 4–9 cm), **pillow-shaped** with rounded corners and
  slightly convex faces; surface divided by incised straight lines into boxes ("cases"); numbers sit
  in the cases. Unfired, sun-dried clay: grey-brown to buff, darker while damp.
- [D] Several counting systems by commodity (sexagesimal, bisexagesimal for rations, grain-capacity
  system). Numbers and units were not abstract yet — the same mark meant different amounts for
  different goods.
- [D] Cylinder seals rolled over clay to mark authority/ownership; some tablets carry rollings.
- [I] Token → sealed clay envelope (bulla) → numerical tablet → proto-cuneiform (Schmandt-Besserat).
  Influential and partly contested. Say "scholars think", never "this is how writing began".
- [D] Coins do not exist (first coins: Lydia, c. 7th–6th century BCE).
- [I] Weighed silver as a measure of value is attested later (3rd millennium onward). Don't show it.
- [I] "Kushim" on a barley tablet may be a personal name or an office. Say "possibly".

## Architecture and interiors
- [D] Mud brick (sun-dried, some baked), mud plaster, reed, palm wood, bitumen.
- [D] Eanna precinct: monumental tripartite buildings, **clay-cone mosaics** (red/black/white cone
  heads pressed into walls) — for wide shots only, later in the episode.
- [D] Raised terrace temple (the "White Temple" on the Anu terrace, slightly earlier). A platform,
  not a stepped tower.
- [P] Working rooms: plain mud-plastered walls, packed-earth floor, reed mats, reed-screen doorways.
- [X] **Large stepped ziggurats** (Ur-Nammu's at Ur is c. 2100 BCE), columns, arches with keystones,
  glazed brick (Ishtar Gate is c. 575 BCE), anything "Babylon".

## Objects
- [D] **Bevelled-rim bowls**: coarse, chaff-tempered, thick-walled, mould-made, conical, slanted
  bevelled rim, rough outside, roughly 15–20 cm across. Found by the thousand.
- [I] Bevelled-rim bowls as ration measures — a hypothesis. Show them; don't narrate a function.
- [D] Barley (hulled), emmer, dates, beer, sheep, goats, cattle, fish, wool, flax.
- [D] Stone cylinder seals, small (a few cm), pierced lengthwise, carved with animals/processions.
- [D] Copper (arsenical) tools exist but are scarce; stone, bone, reed, clay are everyday.
- [X] Iron, glass, coins, paper, papyrus scrolls, metal pens, candles, horses, camels, chickens.
- [X] Wheeled carts in shot — early evidence is thin; avoid.

## People and clothing
- [D] Warka Vase (c. 3200–3000 BCE) and Uruk-period seals: male workers bare-chested in a wrap
  skirt; the "priest-king" bearded, headband, net-patterned skirt. Women in long wrapped garments.
- [P] Fabrics: plain undyed wool and linen, natural off-white/brown; bare feet or simple sandals.
- [X] The tufted fleece "kaunakes" skirt (Early Dynastic, c. 2600 BCE), turbans, Egyptian kilts and
  headdresses, Greek drapery, rings/watches, dyed bright colours as everyday wear.
- [X] Nude figures (they are on the Warka Vase; we don't show them).

## Light and nature
- [P] Hot, dry, bright; hard morning/late sun, dust. Date palms, reeds, marsh nearby.
- [P] Interiors lit by doorway daylight. Oil lamps exist but avoid flame-lit interiors in daytime.
