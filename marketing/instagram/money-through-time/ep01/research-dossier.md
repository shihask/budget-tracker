# EP01 — The First Account · Research dossier

Uruk, southern Mesopotamia, c. 3200 BCE. **For review — not a screenplay.** Compiled 2026-11-10.

**Labels.** **DOCUMENTED** — attested by excavated objects, texts or a clear scholarly consensus, with a
source. **DEBATED** — an interpretation scholars argue about, including majority views; say "scholars
think", never state it as fact. **DRAMATISATION** — our invention for the screen; never presented as
history. Items marked *(verify)* rest on a secondary or catalogue source and should be checked in the
primary publication before they go into narration.

**Dating.** The archaeological phases are Uruk IV (c. 3350–3200 BCE) and Uruk III / Jemdet Nasr
(c. 3200–3000 BCE). c. 3200 BCE sits on their boundary. Absolute dates are approximate and the
chronology is itself debated, so narration should say "about 5,000 years ago" or "around 3200 BCE".

## 3. Why records were needed

| # | Finding | Label | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| 3.1 | The earliest Uruk texts are overwhelmingly administrative: who received, delivered or owes what, how much, over what period | DOCUMENTED | [B1]; [S3]; CDLI [S1] |
| 3.2 | Writing emerged as a tool of institutional bookkeeping — not for literature, letters, law or history | DEBATED as a cause, but the **majority view**; the evidence (purpose of the earliest texts) is documented | [B1]; [S3] |
| 3.3 | Records let an administrator track quantities beyond memory, across many people and months, and hold officials accountable (a "final account" over 37 months, see 4.6) | DOCUMENTED (such accounts exist) | CDLI P005340 [S5] |

## 4. The earliest proto-cuneiform tablets

| # | Finding | Label | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| 4.1 | Corpus: over 6,000 proto-cuneiform tablets and fragments known; nearly 5,000 from the German excavations at Uruk | DOCUMENTED (figures as of the cited dates) | Englund 2001/2011 [S3]; CDLI [S1] |
| 4.2 | Roughly 85% administrative, 15% lexical — an average; almost no lexical texts in Uruk IV, more in Uruk III | DOCUMENTED (Englund's own caveat) | [S3] |
| 4.3 | **Found as rubbish.** The archaic tablets came from secondary deposits — discarded in a dump later used as fill to level the terrace under the Archaic Level III buildings. They cannot be tied to the building they were used in | DOCUMENTED | CDLI wiki [S1], [S2] |
| 4.4 | Numbers are well understood; around 60 number signs in a dozen-plus metrological systems (counts differ: 13 or 15) | DOCUMENTED | Damerow & Englund in [B1] |
| 4.5 | **The same numeral meant different amounts depending on what was counted** (discrete objects sexagesimal; grain-based rations bisexagesimal; grain volume the ŠE system); this misled scholars for decades | DOCUMENTED | [B1]; Proto-cuneiform numerals summaries *(verify wording)* |
| 4.6 | MSVO 3, 29: "5617 1/5 [units] … barley … 37 months … Kushim's final account" (Englund's translation on CDLI) | DOCUMENTED (tablet and reading) | CDLI P005340 [S5] |
| 4.7 | "Kushim" is the first named person in history | DEBATED — the sign group KU.ŠIM may be a personal name, an official's title, or an institution. Popular books state it as fact; we do not | [S5]; CDLB 2003-4 [S6] |
| 4.8 | Lexical lists catalogue signs by theme — officials/professions, animals, vessels, possibly metals | DOCUMENTED | DCCLT (Veldhuis) [S7] |
| 4.9 | The **Standard Professions list** (ED Lu A) is attested from Uruk IV (oldest exemplar VAT 15003, c. 3200 BCE) and was copied, nearly unchanged, for about a thousand years into Old Babylonian times, at sites from Uruk to Ebla | DOCUMENTED | CDLJ 2003:3 [S9]; [S7] |
| 4.10 | What language the scribes spoke (Sumerian?) | DEBATED — numbers and many signs are understood, but proto-cuneiform records little grammar; the language behind it is disputed | [S3]; [B2] *(verify wording)* |
| 4.11 | Proto-cuneiform is "fully deciphered" | DEBATED — say "partly understood": numbers yes, many signs yes, much of the content still uncertain | [S3] |

## 5. Materials, tools, techniques

| # | Finding | Label | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| 5.1 | Tablets of local clay, unfired (sun-dried); small, pillow-shaped, slightly convex | DOCUMENTED | Met M1/M2; CDLI photos |
| 5.2 | **Numbers impressed** with the round end of a stylus — slanted for a notch, upright for a circle; larger values with a thicker stylus | DOCUMENTED | [B1]; [S3] |
| 5.3 | **Other signs incised** with a pointed stylus as curved line drawings; the wedge-shaped strokes of "cuneiform" come centuries later | DOCUMENTED | [B1]; [S3] |
| 5.4 | Surface divided by ruled lines into cases; entries on the obverse, totals often on the reverse | DOCUMENTED *(verify the reverse-totals pattern in [S3])* | [S3] |
| 5.5 | Styluses were reed (or bone/wood) | DEBATED for this date — reed is the standard reconstruction; organic tools don't survive | — |
| 5.6 | Cylinder-seal rollings on some tablets (e.g. Met 1988.433.1, barley account with a hunting scene) | DOCUMENTED | [M1] |
| 5.7 | Before tablets: hollow clay envelopes (**bullae**) holding small clay tokens, sometimes impressed on the outside; then **numerical tablets** (numbers only), then numbers + one or two signs — at Uruk, Susa, Habuba Kabira, Godin Tepe and others | DOCUMENTED (the objects and their rough sequence) | Schmandt-Besserat 1992 [B3]; [B1] |

| 5.9 | **Complex tokens → word signs → writing** (the full token theory) | **DEBATED — significantly criticised.** Critics: Zimansky 1993, Michalowski 1993, Friberg 1994, Englund 1998. Never present as settled | Zimansky 1993 [S15]; Ferrara [S16]; [B3] |

## 7. Documented vs. debated — at a glance

**Safe to state (DOCUMENTED):** large institutions at Uruk counted grain, animals, people and goods on
clay; numbers were impressed, signs incised; most early tablets are accounts; the tablets were found
in rubbish fill; numbers changed meaning with the commodity; 30-day administrative months; cylinder
seals marked responsibility; bullae, tokens and numerical tablets came before; the professions list
was copied for ~1,000 years; there were no coins.

**Say "scholars think" (DEBATED):** writing was invented *because of* accounting (majority view);
tokens became writing; bevelled-rim bowls held rations; "Kushim" is a person; Uruk was the first
city / had a king; colonies; slaves vs. dependent workers; the scribes' language; which god a temple
served; exact population; exact dates.

**Avoid entirely:** dialogue in Sumerian; named individuals other than the sign group "Kushim" (with
its caveat); invented events (a famine, a theft, a dispute) presented as history; tablets with readable
wedge script; coins or silver payments; ziggurats.

## 8. Details that could create suspense (all DOCUMENTED unless marked)

1. **The oldest accounts survived because someone threw them away.** Discarded in a dump, used as
   building fill — and so preserved for 5,000 years.
2. **A number that changes its meaning.** The same mark meant one quantity for sheep and another for
   barley; modern scholars were misled for decades.
3. **A name — or not.** "Kushim's final account" of barley over 37 months: a person, a title, or an
   office? (DEBATED — the open question is the suspense.)
4. **People counted like goods.** Accounts that list men and women by sex and age with grain amounts
   beside them — and no word for "work".
5. **A list that refused to change.** A professions list from c. 3200 BCE copied almost word for word
   for a thousand years.
6. **A temple destroyed on purpose.** The Stone-Cone Temple was ritually demolished and its contents
   buried (verify).
7. **A vase stolen in 2003.** The Warka Vase, from the same precinct, ripped from its case and handed
   back in pieces two months later. (Modern — a framing device, flagged.)
8. **A race on two continents.** Egyptian labels at Abydos of about the same age (DEBATED priority).
9. **We can count their barley but can't be sure what language they spoke.** (DEBATED.)

## Opening hooks (proposals — no invented events)

1. **"The rubbish dump."** Open on a layer of dry fill; a hand brushes out a fragment. *"The oldest
   accounts ever found were not kept in a vault. They were thrown away."* (DOCUMENTED, 4.3; the
   excavation moment is DRAMATISATION.)
2. **"Before the first poem."** *"Before anyone wrote down a poem, a law or a name, someone wrote down
   barley."* Cut to a reed stylus pressing a notch. (DOCUMENTED, 3.1–3.2, with "scholars think" for the
   why.)
3. **"Who remembers?"** *"Five thousand years ago, a city of tens of thousands handed out grain every
   month. How did anyone remember who had been paid?"* (Question framing; facts 1.2 DEBATED range, 2.6
   DOCUMENTED.)

## Cliffhangers into EP02 (proposals — depend on EP02's topic, not yet decided)

1. **"Barley spoils."** *"Clay could remember a debt. But you can't carry a granary in your hand. What
   could?"* → EP02 on silver weighed as value (a 3rd-millennium development — flagged, 9).
2. **"The name on the tablet."** *"One sign group keeps appearing: Kushim. A man? A title? Whoever it
   was, the tablet says they had to account for every measure."* → EP02 on responsibility, officials
   and debt. (DEBATED, 4.7.)
3. **"The marks grow up."** *"These marks only counted. Within a few centuries they would begin to
   speak — names, laws, promises."* → EP02 on writing beyond accounts / the first contracts.
   (DOCUMENTED, 9.)
